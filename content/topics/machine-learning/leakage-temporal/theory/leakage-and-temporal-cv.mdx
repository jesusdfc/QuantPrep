# Data Leakage & Why Random k-Fold Fails for Time Series

The fastest way to produce a "great" model that fails live is **leakage**: information from
the target or the future sneaking into training.

## Flavors of leakage

- **Target leakage:** a feature is a proxy for the label that would not be available at
  prediction time (e.g. using end-of-day return to predict end-of-day direction).
- **Look-ahead bias:** using data timestamped after the decision point — a price revision,
  a restated fundamental, a label built from future returns aligned to the wrong bar.
- **Preprocessing leakage:** fitting a scaler, imputer, or feature-selection step on the
  **full** dataset before splitting. The test set has then influenced training.
- **Group leakage:** correlated rows (same asset, overlapping windows) split across train
  and test, so the model effectively sees its test data.

## Why random k-fold CV is wrong for time series

Random k-fold shuffles rows, so:

1. **Future leaks into past.** Fold assignment ignores time, so a model trains on data that
   occurs *after* its validation points.
2. **Autocorrelation inflates scores.** Adjacent observations are similar; scattering them
   across folds makes validation points near-duplicates of training points.
3. **Overlapping labels.** If a label spans multiple bars (e.g. next-5-day return), train
   and validation labels overlap in time.

## What to do instead

- **Walk-forward / expanding-window** splits: always train on the past, validate on the
  future.
- **Purging:** drop training samples whose labels overlap the validation window.
- **Embargo:** skip a gap after each validation block so serial correlation cannot bridge it.
- Fit **all** preprocessing inside the training fold only.

## The interview tell

When asked to validate a predictive model on market data, immediately reach for
**temporal** splits and mention purging/embargo. Reaching for `train_test_split(shuffle=True)`
signals you have not internalized the finance-specific traps.
