# Drawdown and Maximum Drawdown

The drawdown is the measure of the decline from a historical peak in some variable (typically
the cumulative profit or total open equity of a financial trading strategy).

Formally, if $X(t),\; t \ge 0$ is a stochastic process with $X(0)=0$, the drawdown at time $T$,
denoted $D(T)$, is defined as

$$
D(T) = \max_{t \in (0,T)} X(t) - X(T).
$$


The maximum drawdown (MDD) up to time $T$ is the maximum of the drawdown over the history of the
variable:

$$
\operatorname{MDD}(T) = \max_{\tau \in (0,T)} \left[\max_{t \in (0,\tau)} X(t) - X(\tau)\right].
$$


There are two main definitions of a drawdown: **how low it goes** (the magnitude — the worst
peak-to-valley loss) and **how long it lasts** (the duration — the length of any peak-to-peak
period, i.e. the time between new equity highs). In finance, the maximum drawdown is used as an
indicator of risk in performance measures such as the Calmar ratio, the Sterling ratio and the
Burke ratio.
