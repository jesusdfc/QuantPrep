# Diversification Techniques in Portfolio Construction

Diversification techniques in portfolio construction aim to reduce unsystematic (idiosyncratic)
risk by combining assets whose returns are not perfectly correlated, so that losses in some
holdings are offset by gains (or smaller losses) in others. The goal is **better risk-adjusted
returns, not necessarily higher absolute returns**.

## Core Techniques

- **Across asset classes**: Stocks (equities), bonds (fixed income), cash/cash equivalents,
  real assets (real estate/REITs, commodities, gold), and alternatives. Classic starting points
  are 60/40 or 70/30 equity/bond mixes; the mix is driven by risk tolerance, horizon, and goals.
- **Within asset classes**: By market capitalization (large/mid/small), style (value/growth),
  sectors/industries, credit quality/duration (for bonds), and geography (domestic vs.
  international / developed vs. emerging).
- **Factor-based**: Exposure to distinct return drivers such as value, momentum, quality,
  low-volatility, size. These often have lower correlations than pure asset-class buckets.
- **Correlation-aware / risk-based allocation**:
  - **Mean-variance optimization (Markowitz)**: Minimize portfolio variance for a target return
    (or maximize Sharpe). Sensitive to estimation error in means and covariances.
  - **Risk parity / equal risk contribution**: Allocate so each asset (or risk factor)
    contributes equally to total portfolio risk rather than equal capital weights. Often
    involves leverage.
  - **Hierarchical Risk Parity (HRP) and related clustering methods**: Group similar assets via
    hierarchical clustering then allocate risk across clusters; more robust when correlations
    are unstable.
  - **Maximum diversification**: Maximize the ratio of weighted average asset volatility to
    portfolio volatility.
  - **Black-Litterman and other Bayesian approaches**: Blend market-implied returns with
    investor views to stabilize estimates.
- **Alternatives and diversifiers**: Trend-following, global macro, market-neutral,
  multi-strategy, commodities, or other low-correlation strategies that perform differently
  across regimes.
- **Practical overlays**: Rebalancing (periodic or threshold-based) to keep risk exposures
  consistent; constraints (position limits, turnover, liquidity); cost-aware optimization; and
  stress-testing / scenario analysis.

## Key caveats

- Correlations rise in crises (diversification benefits can vanish when needed most).
- Estimation error is large with noisy returns data.
- Over-diversification can dilute returns and increase costs.
