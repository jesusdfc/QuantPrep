# Expectation, Conditioning & Tail-Sum Tricks

Most probability interview problems are not about knowing an exotic distribution — they
are about applying a small number of tools quickly and without error.

## 1. Linearity of expectation

For **any** random variables (independent or not):

$$
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_i X_i\right] = \sum_i \mathbb{E}[X_i].
$$

This is the single most useful identity. It lets you compute the expected number of
"events" by summing indicator probabilities, even when the events are highly dependent.

> **Example.** Expected number of fixed points of a random permutation of $n$ items.
> Let $X_i = 1$ if item $i$ is fixed. Then $\mathbb{E}[X_i] = 1/n$, so
> $\mathbb{E}[\sum_i X_i] = n \cdot \tfrac{1}{n} = 1$, regardless of $n$.

## 2. Conditioning (first-step analysis)

The **law of total expectation**:

$$
\mathbb{E}[X] = \mathbb{E}\big[\,\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]\,\big].
$$

For "expected time until..." problems, condition on the first step and set up a
recurrence. Define states, write one equation per state, and solve the linear system.

> **Pattern.** Let $E$ be the expected number of trials to reach a goal. Condition on the
> first outcome; the process either finishes or restarts from a known state.

## 3. The tail-sum formula

For a **non-negative integer** random variable:

$$
\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{P}(X \ge k).
$$

For a non-negative continuous variable: $\mathbb{E}[X] = \int_0^\infty \mathbb{P}(X > t)\,dt$.
This converts an awkward expectation into a sum/integral of survival probabilities — ideal
for "maximum of..." and "time until..." questions.

## 4. Law of total variance

$$
\operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}\big[\operatorname{Var}(X \mid Y)\big] + \operatorname{Var}\big(\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]\big).
$$

Read as **within-group** variance plus **between-group** variance. It appears constantly
in mixture and hierarchical problems.

## Why this matters for quant interviews

Interviewers care less about the final number and more about whether you **set up the
right decomposition instantly** and reason cleanly out loud. Practice until conditioning
and linearity are reflexes.
